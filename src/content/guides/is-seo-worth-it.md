It depends on whether people search for what the business sells, and that is checkable in about ten minutes.

## How to check

Open Google's Keyword Planner or a similar free tool and search for the actual service, in the actual area served — "plumber Kilimani," not "plumbing services," if that is genuinely how a customer would search. A near-zero result across every reasonable phrasing of the service means there is little to no search demand to capture, regardless of how good the eventual SEO work would be. A visible, even modest, volume means real demand exists and the rest of this question is about payback, not existence.

## When the answer is no

Three situations where SEO is not worth pursuing, at least for now: no search demand for the actual service in the actual area, as checked above; unit economics too thin to absorb a months-long payback period before the investment returns anything, as covered in [how long does SEO take to work?](/guides/how-long-seo-takes); or an existing referral or repeat-customer channel already running at full capacity, where more visibility would create demand the business cannot currently serve.

## When the answer is yes

Real, checkable search demand for the service, an economics that can absorb a payback period measured in months rather than days, and either spare capacity to serve new customers or a plan to grow into it. A business meeting all three has a genuine case for SEO, independent of how urgently a provider might frame the decision.

## Is this for a business my size?

Size matters less than the three conditions above. A small business with real, checkable search demand, sound unit economics, and room to serve new customers is a better SEO candidate than a larger business missing any one of the three.
