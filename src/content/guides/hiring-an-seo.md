Hiring for SEO is a genuine choice set, not a single decision. The options are doing nothing, doing it yourself, hiring a freelancer, hiring an agency, or bringing someone in-house — and the honest case for each depends on the business, not on which option sounds more serious.

## The four options, and where each wins

**Doing nothing** is a real option, not a failure to act, when [is-seo-worth-it](/guides/is-seo-worth-it) comes back negative — no search demand for what the business sells, or an existing channel already at capacity.

**DIY** suits a business with more time than budget, a straightforward local presence, and an owner willing to learn Google Business Profile and basic on-page work. [doing-seo-yourself](/guides/doing-seo-yourself) sets out exactly what that covers.

**A freelancer** suits a business with a defined, scoped need — an audit, a migration, ongoing content — and a low enough budget that agency overheads do not make sense. Quality varies more than with an agency, because there is no second person checking the work.

**An agency** suits a business that wants a team, continuity if one person leaves, and a wider skill set than one freelancer is likely to hold — technical, content, and local all covered. It costs more, and the reasons why are covered in [seo-cost-kenya](/guides/seo-cost-kenya).

**An in-house hire** suits a business large enough to keep someone fully occupied on SEO alone, which in the Kenyan SME context is a small minority of businesses considering any of the other four options.

## What a scope of work should specify

A scope of work should name the deliverables, not the activity. "Monthly SEO" with no defined deliverables is the single most common way SEO budgets get wasted, because there is nothing in the agreement to measure against and nothing to point to when six months pass with no visible change.

A workable scope specifies: what gets audited and when, what content gets produced and how it gets approved, what technical fixes are in scope versus billed separately, and what reporting looks like — ideally direct Search Console access, not a branded PDF.

## Retainer, project, or audit-only

| Model | Best for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Audit-only | A one-time diagnosis, or checking a claim before committing to more | Vague deliverables with no prioritised findings |
| Project | A defined piece of work — a migration, a content batch, a technical fix list | Scope creep with no adjustment to the price |
| Monthly retainer | Ongoing work where the site and market shift enough to need continuous attention | No defined deliverables, reviewed above |

Audit-only is the lowest-commitment way to test whether a provider's diagnosis matches what an independent check would find.

## Can I just do this myself?

The honest test for whether to hire at all: does the business have more available time than budget, and is the work in question genuinely learnable in that time. Google Business Profile setup, basic on-page fixes, and a defined content calendar are learnable. Diagnosing a crawl problem, structuring a polyhierarchic content plan, or negotiating a technical migration without breaking existing rankings generally are not, inside the time a business owner actually has free.

[Can you do SEO yourself?](/guides/doing-seo-yourself) walks through the split in detail, including where DIY goes wrong.
