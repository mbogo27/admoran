Partly "nobody knows yet," and that is a more credible answer than a confident prediction in either direction. What follows separates what has changed from what is still speculation.

## What has demonstrably changed

Answer surfaces — AI Overviews, chat assistants answering search-shaped questions directly — now sit ahead of or instead of the traditional results list for a meaningful share of searches. Zero-click behaviour, where a searcher gets an answer without visiting any site, has increased as a direct consequence. Citation — being named as a source within an assembled answer — has become a visibility channel in its own right, separate from ranking position in the traditional list.

## What has not changed

Being crawlable is still the precondition for everything else; an AI system summarising the web still has to be able to access a page to draw from it. Being clear — stating claims directly, in extractable form — was already good practice before AI Overviews existed and remains good practice now. Being corroborated — having a claim supported by other credible sources — was already how trust worked in search and continues to be how it works in assembled answers.

## The two-sided question

AI is both the thing changing how search results are presented, and a tool some businesses now use to do SEO work itself — drafting content, analysing a site, summarising a competitor's approach. The first side is the subject of this guide. The second is a separate, ordinary question about which tools to use for which tasks, and does not by itself answer whether AI is "replacing" the discipline.

## How long before I see anything?

What to act on now: the things that were already correct practice — clear, direct content, sound technical SEO, structured data. See [structured data: what it is and what it does](/guides/structured-data-basics). What to watch rather than act on yet: platform-specific citation tactics aimed at a particular AI system's current behaviour, because that behaviour has changed multiple times already and is likely to keep changing.

<!-- frame:risk -->
## Could this damage my site or waste my money?

Two risks, in opposite directions. Over-correcting — dropping established, working practice to chase an unstable and unpublished target — wastes effort on tactics that may not survive the next change. Doing nothing — treating this shift as hype and ignoring it entirely — risks missing the part that is real: citation as a genuine, growing visibility channel. The safer position sits between the two: treat AI visibility as continuous with existing good SEO practice, not as a reason to abandon it or as a separate discipline requiring separate spend.
