Google Business Profile is the single most actionable piece of local SEO available to a Kenyan business, and it is possible to work through the whole thing in an afternoon and see a measurable difference within weeks.

## Claiming and verifying

Search for the business name on Google Maps first — a profile may already exist, created automatically or by a previous owner, and claiming an existing one is faster than creating a duplicate. Google verifies ownership by postcard, phone, email, or video, depending on the business category and location.

Verification failure is common in Kenya for businesses without conventional signage or a formal postal address. Where postcard verification fails or the address does not match a recognisable format, video verification — walking the camera around the premises, showing signage, stock, and the surrounding area — is usually the most reliable fallback, and Google's support flow offers it directly once postcard verification has failed once.

## Category selection

Primary category is the highest-leverage single field on the entire profile, because it is the strongest signal Google uses to decide which searches the business is relevant for. Choose the most specific accurate category available, not the broadest one — "Plumber" rather than "Contractor," if plumbing is the actual service.

Secondary categories add coverage for genuinely offered adjacent services, not for services the business does not actually provide. Adding categories to chase more search visibility, for services not actually delivered, produces mismatched traffic that does not convert and can trigger a review if flagged.

## Service areas versus a physical address

A business with a public storefront should list a physical address and let it show on the map. A business that travels to the customer — a mobile technician, a cleaning service, a contractor — should set a service area instead of, or in addition to, a public address, and hide the address if there is no public-facing premises. Listing a home address as a public storefront for a service-area business is a common and avoidable mistake.

## Photos, hours, products, and Q&A

Photos should be recent, well-lit, and specific to the business — the premises, the team, the product or work itself, not stock imagery. Hours should be accurate and updated for public holidays, since incorrect hours are one of the more common causes of a lost customer who shows up to a closed business.

The **products and services** section and the **Q&A section** are frequently left empty. Q&A is public and anyone can post a question — a business that never answers its own Q&A section is leaving space for an incorrect or unhelpful answer to sit there instead. Seeding it with the genuinely common questions, answered directly, closes that gap.

## Review requests and responding to reviews

Ask for reviews directly, right after a positive interaction, using the short link Google generates for the profile. Respond to every review, positive or negative — a considered response to a negative review is often more persuasive to a future customer than the review itself.

## Does this actually work?

Business Profile insights report **discovery** searches — someone found the business by searching a category or service term, not the name — separately from **direct** searches, where the searcher already knew the name. A rising discovery share over the weeks after a profile is completed and optimised is the clearest, most directly attributable signal that the work moved something, because it specifically measures being found by people who did not already know the business existed.

## Can I just do this myself?

Yes, entirely. Every step in this guide is within reach of a business owner directly, with no specialist tool and no ongoing fee. This is the most fully DIY-able piece of SEO work covered anywhere in this set of guides, and treating it as something that requires paying someone else is usually an unnecessary cost.
